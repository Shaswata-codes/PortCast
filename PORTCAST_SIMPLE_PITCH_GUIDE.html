2️⃣ What is the “Haldia Sandheads” Port Problem?

🛬 The “Landing a Jumbo Jet on a Grass Runway” Analogy: “Haldia Port in West Bengal is very shallow—only 8.5 meters deep. Bringing a massive 180,000-tonne ship into Haldia is like trying to land an Airbus A380 on a tiny village grass runway—it will crash and get stuck in the mud. Ships are forced to stop miles offshore at a place called ‘Sandheads’ to slowly transfer cargo into small boats (called Lighterage), which costs an extra $5.25 per tonne and wastes 4 days. PortCast warns charterers instantly and recommends deepwater Dhamra Port instead, saving $210,000.”

3️⃣ What is the “Vessel Capacity Utilization Bar”?

🚌 The “Hiring a 50-Seater Luxury Bus for 2 People” Analogy: “If you have 40,000 tonnes of coal, but you hire a massive 180,000-tonne Capesize ship, you are paying fuel and daily rent for 140,000 tonnes of empty air! That’s why the capacity bar shows Rose/Red (22%). PortCast automatically selects a Supramax ship (58,000 tonnes), turning the bar Emerald Green (85% efficiency) so you don’t waste money.”

4️⃣ What are the “What-If” Sliders?

🚗 The “Uber Surge Pricing Simulator” Analogy: “On a rainy Friday night, Uber prices surge because of rain and traffic jams. In shipping, freight prices surge when Bunker Fuel (oil) spikes or ports get jammed with queues (congestion). Our What-If Sliders let managers drag the fuel price or delay days to simulate the surge BEFORE they sign a contract.”

💡 THE 5 MAJOR TERMS EXPLAINED IN 10 WORDS OR LESS

  1. Dry Bulk: Loose commodities like Coal, Iron Ore, and Grain moved in giant ship holds.
  2. Charterer: The company renting the ship (e.g. Tata Steel or NTPC importing coal).
  3. Fixture: The final contract booking date for hiring a ship.
  4. Draft: How deep the ship sits in the water. (Big ship = deep draft = needs deep port).
  5. Demurrage: The $18,000/day late penalty you pay if your ship is stuck waiting outside a crowded port.
